One workspace for AI coding

Your accounts. Your machines. One setup for the whole team.

Coding Lab brings together the AI subscriptions you already pay for and the computers you already own, then gives your team one shared place to work from, wherever they are.

01

Use every AI account, subscription and local model

Connect Claude and Codex accounts on any plan, personal or company, plus your own models running on company GPU servers. Use them side by side, and switch per conversation when one hits its limit or another fits the task better.

  • Personal, corporate and local models in one list
  • Pick the account and model for each conversation
  • See usage per account, so nobody runs dry mid-task

02

Turn your own machines into virtual workers

A Mac mini on the shelf, a Linux box, a cloud VM: install the worker and it runs sessions for the team. Your code stays on hardware you control, and nothing stops when you close your laptop.

  • Runs keep going with the lid closed
  • See each machine’s load and what’s running on it
  • No extra compute bill, just the hardware you have
03

The whole team works in the same project checkout

Set the project up once: repositories, branches, tools, environment and instructions. Everyone’s sessions start from that same checkout, so there are no “works on my machine” surprises and handing work over is easy.

  • New teammates are productive on day one
  • Pick up a teammate’s conversation where they left it
  • Each session gets its own worktree, so nobody steps on anyone

How it works

From a sentence to pushed code, without babysitting it.

01Connect your AI accountsAdd the Claude and Codex accounts and subscriptions you already have, personal or company, as many as you like.
02Add your machines as workersInstall the worker app on a Mac mini, a Linux box or a cloud VM. Sessions run there, not on your laptop.
03Set the project up onceRepositories, tools, environment and instructions in one checkout that the whole team shares.
04Work from anywhereDescribe the task on the web or your phone. It runs in its own worktree and commits or asks first, by your rules.
